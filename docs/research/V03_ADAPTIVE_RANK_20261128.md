## S02 — causal transport error, not uniform-density error
Let mu_k be a positive finite marked measure of admission time in seconds and mass in vehicles, including uniform densities and Dirac atoms. A bin I=(l,r] (first includes its left endpoint) maps each x to r; nu_k=(T_r)_#mu_k. T_r(x)>=x, and the bin preserves mass and birth first-moment integral independently per mark. For any s inside a bin, F_mu(s)-F_nu(s)=mu(I intersect (-infty,s]); it is nonnegative and vanishes at right boundaries. The exact local supremum over s<r is mu(I)-mu({r}), not one quarter of bin mass. In particular, moving an atom near l to r can attain the whole mass error. The previous diagnostic named generic_prefix_bound used M/4 for a right-point projection, which is not valid. The earlier 1/4 theorem for averaging a bounded continuous density on an interval remains correct and is a different operator.
On the line, for measures of equal finite mass, the nonnegative transport delay integral D=int (T_r(x)-x)dmu equals int(F_mu-F_nu) ds by Tonelli (each x contributes a rectangle from x to r). It is also the minimum cost over couplings: |y-x|>=y-x, and this coupling attains equality, hence W1=D (unnormalized veh*s). For any L-Lipschitz g, |int g dnu-int g dmu|<=L D. With a nonnegative birth-moment measure the same identity holds in veh*s^2, though this implementation constrains mass delay, not birth-density delay. Rank/order across different marks inside one bin is not retained; future FIFO network equivalence is NOT implied. Protected boundaries: receiving cell, common tick and target, route/offset and owner, class; point atoms can move only within that tick except sparse exact point mode.
Construct adaptive coarsening of a finest grid of <=16 intervals. Fine cell j has mass m_j and delay d_j=int(r_j-x)dmu. For a merged contiguous block i..j the exact causal delay is sum_{k=i}^j [d_k + m_k(r_j-r_k)]. Enforce each block D_ij <= theta*dt*sum m_k, hence total delay <=theta*dt*M. Dynamic programming over ordered boundaries, dp[j]=min_{i<j: admissible(i,j)}(1+dp[i]), obtains minimal bin count among partitions of this fixed fine grid; no assumption of monotone feasibility. Tie break by total delay then first predecessor encountered. Fine widths <=theta*dt when theta>=1/max_bins and duration<=dt, so fine solution exists in real arithmetic. In binary64 check finite accumulators and recompute achieved delay after emission; this is numerically checked with documented tolerance, not directed-rounding certificate.
