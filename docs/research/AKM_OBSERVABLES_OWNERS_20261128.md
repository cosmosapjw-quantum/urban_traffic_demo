## S01–S02. Derived discriminator: ballistic versus mixed residence

For a single homogeneous cell, constant velocity U, no reaction, no receiving constraint, and preloaded unit pulse at t=0, AKM-A's proportional flux with Courant number a=U dt/L∈(0,1] gives m_n=(1-a)^n. Exit N has geometric law P(N=n)=a(1-a)^(n-1), n≥1. The time T=N dt has E[T]=L/U, Var[T]=(L/U)^2(1-a). At a=1 it exactly translates the pulse (also the free-flow CTM/LTM limit with aligned mesh). At dt→0, fixed L, the limit is exponential with the SAME nonzero mean and standard deviation L/U. Hence time refinement at fixed cell length cannot recover ballistic passage CDF. At length L divided into k identical cells, independent geometric residence convolution has E[T]=L/U and Var[T]=(L/U)^2(1-a)/k. Spatial refinement k→∞ concentrates the passage in L². This is a constructive model/discretization diagnosis; not a universal error bound with nonlinear congestion, kinetic reaction, or source admission. For the registered pulse no source admission offset is included. The low-density ballistic CDF is a legitimate independent oracle only for identical monokinetic drift.

Second discriminator: velocity-selective advection. Let a normalized-speed positive measure on [0,1] have M,J,Z (mass veh; J,Z also veh). At CFL c=U dt/L, an upwind velocity-dependent transfer takes fraction c*u at speed u; total outgoing M_out=c J, J_out=c Z, Z_out=c W (third moment). For proportional transfer M_out=c J but J_out=c J²/M, underestimating outgoing momentum by c(Z−J²/M)=c M Var(u), with equality only monokinetic or c=0. Third moment is not determined by M,J,Z; rather than discard the first two moments, reconstruct a positive two-atom quadrature at 0 and v=Z/J with moving mass J²/Z (J,Z>0) and stationary remainder. Then M,J,Z exactly match, W*=Z²/J, and CFL c≤1 ensures both transferred and retained measures positive. Cauchy-Schwarz J²≤MZ and Z≤J guarantee the construction. J=0 implies Z=0 (all stopped). W* is the sharp lower bound W≥Z²/J by Cauchy-Schwarz on u^(1/2),u^(3/2), attained by this two-atom law. The construction is not a claim that the physical distribution has two speeds. Can apply independently to each sigma; a common acceptance scale multiplies c. Birth M_out/M allocation is an additional independence closure, not exact birth-speed correlation. This is an optional D04 diagnostic, not a silent substitution for A. Upwind flux and source do not commute with moment projection in general; this motivates D05.
Counterexample: same marked total masses and same velocity moments, but head A destined to closed branch followed by B destined to open branch versus BA. Strict ordered FIFO gives 0 vs up to mass B; mixed donor-wide scaling predicts 0 for both. Lane pockets can justify a different partial FIFO physical law, not universal ordering equivalence.

## S09. Sharp uncertainty interval and physical cross-owner review

For M>0 and 0<J<M, applying Cauchy–Schwarz with measure (1-u)dmu gives (J-Z)^2 <= (M-J)(Z-W). Therefore Z²/J <= W <= Z-(J-Z)^2/(M-J). The upper bound is attained by atoms at a=(J-Z)/(M-J) and 1 with masses (M-J)/(1-a) and the remainder. Writing p=J/M and v=Z/M-p², interval width divided by M is v(1-v/[p(1-p)]) <= p(1-p)/4 <= 1/16. Equality at p=1/2, Z/M=3/8. Thus the unclosed outgoing second moment at fixed c has uncertainty at most c M/16, a sharp local bound. It is not a global error bound, and a FIFO model cannot be identified by this velocity interval. Degenerate J=0 or J=M fixes W=0 or M, respectively. Independent 4096 rational cases (4019 nondegenerate) and sharp equality verified.
