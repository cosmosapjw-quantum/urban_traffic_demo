## Finite-window proposition

Fix a finite sequence of external inputs on a time window. A physical bin is a specific ordered head or interval component in a specific road group, with fixed class and future routing behaviour until the next synchronization. The state retains the full eligibility, capacity, receiving, signal, ordering and motion data that determine service, including the marks on which those controls depend. An induction on events requires that the physical controls are determined from this retained state and the external inputs. A tag-specific control read is a synchronization boundary unless its exact input has already been evaluated. Positive masses are counted in vehicles and fractional service is dimensionless. The model works over the reals.

Let each bin hold tagged amounts in columns of a matrix X. Each column may denote mass of a named tag, its birth first moment in vehicle seconds, tag-associated persons or cargo in kg. Variable bus occupancy and analogous in-window mark changes need their own explicit source terms. A proportional transfer of a fraction f of bin i to j acts on every column by the same sparse linear map A. It leaves the fraction 1−f in i and adds f times the old i to j. A sink has no receiving bin; its mass is recorded at removal. A multiway split can be represented as a nonnegative local matrix; successive elementary moves must use fractions conditional on the remaining amount. External releases can have dedicated immutable input coordinates at the times they become eligible.

Further assume each point of the control trace uses the exact physical projection of the eager state. The forward reduced computation produces the same control trace: equal controls choose equal event clocks, legal destinations and service fraction, and summing the tagged transport commutes with the transfer within each bin. Induction starts at the equality of initial projected states. A whole reduced engine would also need this commutation for the interval-slab construction, finite response and every external coupling.

Choose an observation with terminal bin weights λ and a scalar reward h per unit of a given transfer. Work backwards on the *realized* trace. For each i→j transfer, replace the sender coefficient by

    λ_before[i] = (1−f) λ_after[i] + f (λ_after[j] + h).

Other coefficients stay unchanged. For a sink use λ_after[j]=0. Indeed the coefficient of the pre-event i column in the weighted post-event state plus reward has exactly this value. Iteration telescopes, so the observation for every tag and every passive mark is the weighted sum of original source rows using the *same* coefficients. This reconstructs tagged attribution for a realized state. Differentiating traffic dynamics with respect to demand would also differentiate controls, demand and switching times.

Given N initial or injected tagged rows, E sparse constant-arity transfers, B physical bin indices, q observed weight patterns and Z requested scalar output values, the backward postprocessing takes O(q(E+B+N)+Z) real arithmetic operations. The tape and immutable roots need O(E+B+N) slots. Outputs can be streamed a pattern at a time. Forward aggregate physical construction and any source-to-physical-bin partitions are additional costs. Explicit event histories may contain Θ(NE) tuples on a repeatedly served mixed head. Materializing every tuple still costs its output size. Preserving its ability to be expanded while postponing that expansion can reduce the cost of operational queries. No bit-equivalence to the native binary64 operation order is implied.

## Car egress and observability

For each car tag z let its egress delay law put weight p(z,d) at d seconds. For a deadline T and a road exit at time t_e, the fraction of that exit due by T is

    K(e,z) = Σ_d 1[t_e ≤ T−d] p(z,d).

Thus one backward sweep per distinct prefix of *actual sink times* suffices for due amounts for every tag at that deadline. For each z combine those per-tag weights with its p(z,d). Equal induced prefixes can share a sweep even when their nominal delay values differ. For a finite table with m distinct prefixes the rank of K is at most m. This bound can be attained: use deterministic tag delays selecting m distinct nonempty nested event prefixes. Successive differences of their indicator vectors have disjoint, nonempty supports, giving m independent vectors. More generally any exact separable decomposition of the requested payoff table with q terms gives q reverse sweeps. The matrix-rank bound counts separable linear representations, with no universal lower bound on algorithms that exploit a different structure.

The native `NativeJourneyCore::finish` builds a row for each emitted road sink and each atom of its egress law. Source retirement uses a tolerance-adjusted due-time frontier and writes a time-resolved receipt at max(previous tick time, event end plus delay). The exact-real due-mass proposition bounds one read at one deadline. Reproducing the native history requires all its requested receipt times, their declared order and its own binary64 due predicate. Native bus service already tests a per-tag cumulative sink threshold at tick end. That cumulative amount can be recovered for each bus tag with one constant sink payoff for the tick; install the resulting threshold state before it affects the next road input. A single equal-total observer is insufficient when two tag mixtures place different bus mass on opposite sides of the threshold.

## Endogenous FIFO experiment

The reference has two initially ready mixed senders with overlapping tag sets. A weighted max–min junction (priority 1:2) admits one downstream mixed cohort per arrival epoch. Its receiving capacity is bounded by free storage, and a time-varying downstream sink retires the earliest ready cohort first with partial service. Time advances in one-second increments from absolute time 20 s. The eager routine computes controls from its tagged rows. The reduced routine evaluates the same traffic law using only bin masses and live FIFO order. Controls and source data agree for every tested event. The fixture retains route eligibility throughout each head, and provides mass, birth first moments, and tag-dependent persons and cargo marks. Finite-response road motion and delayed land-use feedback lie outside this fixture.

The four sizes (tags per incoming head and physical seconds) are 8, 24, 48 and 96. The programme compares all tagwise outputs for seven payoff masks, including three deadline cuts for tag-dependent mixtures over delay atoms 1, 3 and 7 s. It independently constructs the eager delayed due amounts by enumerating each sink and delay atom, and tests these against the backward cutoff combination. At 96 tags per sender and 96 s, both forward control traces have 239 transfers. The eager history contains 27,696 tagged event tuples. The backward computation consumes 192 immutable source rows, the 239 transfer descriptors and 1,673 scalar backward updates. All tag ledgers and due-mass checks agree in exact Fraction arithmetic. Output digests, timings and smaller cases are in the companion JSON. Timing comparisons cover these Python fixtures only; eager storage includes explicit event history.

For the full candidate, instrument one real original tick for the number of synchronization barriers, distinct physical bins, legally distinct continuation groups, distinct sink-time prefixes induced by egress delays and actual requests for full per-tag history. Compare output enumeration and tape costs using those measurements. If many tags require distinct internal future branches, refine their physical bins before the branch. If many history times are observed, their reconstruction cost must remain in the accounting. The candidate may be deployed on a causal subwindow while preserving a native fallback at the first nonlinear tag-dependent read.
