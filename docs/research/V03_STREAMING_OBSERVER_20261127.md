## S02 — retirement pushforward and transactional finite-memory theorem (derived)
Let pending egress be a positive marked measure on (leg l, b, r=b+d), mass m in carrier units, b,r seconds; the fixed release grid is T_k=T_0+k dt. A future pending row at committed clock T_0 satisfies r>T_0+eps, eps=1e-10 s, and its retirement index k=min{k>=1:r<=T_k+eps}. Then r>T_(k-1)+eps and the completion mark phi=max(T_(k-1),r)=r. For newly introduced rows retired within their creation tick, use phi=max(T_(k-1),r) before aggregation; do not commute max with an average. Refine (leg, k) by all protected future child eligibility cuts of phi (different dwells, bus/service observers), and by any other required mark. In a fixed pending cell, (b,r) satisfy b<=r, b<=T_0, and the right-closed readiness and eligibility inequalities. Positive mass barycentres preserve these linear inequalities, total mass M, first birth moment B_b and completion moment B_phi, hence retirement increments and all fixed child eligibility queries and completion-age moment. For already pending cells use representative b=B_b/M, delay=B_phi/M-b. For ready-now rows use direct retirement to (M,B_phi), not representative delay. In binary64, recheck original r=b+d and rounded addition predicates, reject boundary crossings; reassociated mass/moment accumulation is a distinct research model. No compression of entry atoms solely by ready clock: road offers use mass*birth and ordinal/tag dependent service, not merely total mass.
For hidden private children, all current marks satisfy phi+d_c<=first profile start and no service observer exists. Then the cumulative state (M,B) is closed under positive arrivals: (M,B)+(sum m_i,sum m_i phi_i); at an observation u>=start_c, all old atoms are eligible. Induction over ticks: unchanged retirement mass increments, hidden child release stays zero until profile start, then old part eligible in full; preserve age integral u M-B. If hidden condition fails, stop merging new rows; old summary remains sufficient for the originally locked schedule, and later rows use original grid. This is a finite-memory positive-measure evolution, not merely a data layout. With a Lipschitz observable g of clock, replacing an interval of width w by its weighted centroid changes its integral by <= L M w/2 (Cauchy-Schwarz and Var<=w^2/4); step/indicator queries instead require protected cuts.
